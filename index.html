How this forecast is made

NEREIDA — an integrated hydrological–limnological seasonal forecasting framework for the Ter system (Sau & Susqueda reservoirs), Catalonia

Weather forecasts reach out to about 10 days; climate projections describe decades. This framework fills the gap in between — 1 to 7 months ahead — which is exactly the horizon on which reservoir operators actually plan: how much water will arrive, how low storage will go, and what condition that water will be in when it gets there.

The forecast chain

1 · ForcingSEAS5.1 seasonal ensemble — 51 members from the Copernicus Climate Data Store, re-downloaded every month.
2 · CorrectionBias adjustment & downscaling — every member corrected against the ERA5 reanalysis record.
3 · HydrologyRainfall–runoff over the Ter/Tordera sub-basins (GR4J) → inflows and system storage.
4 · Lake modelGLM 3.1.1 + AED2 — 1-D thermal structure and mixing at Sau and Susqueda, forced by those inflows.
5 · ProductPublished monthly: tercile probabilities and verified skill per lead month, pushed to this viewer.

Reading a chart

Each panel spans the 7-month forecast horizon (t+1 … t+7), and overlays two different things — don't read them as the same shading:

  • Background bands (orange/grey/blue) are climatology, not forecast: the observed below/normal/above-normal ranges for that calendar month, from the 1995–2025 historical record. The dashed lines are the P33/P66 thresholds that define "normal" — they shift month to month, so the bands aren't flat.
  • Navy ribbons are the actual forecast: the 51-member SEAS5.1 ensemble's spread at each lead, shown as a 10–90% envelope (light) and a 33–66% envelope (darker) around the ensemble median (solid navy line). They are not individual member dots — with 51 members plotted for 380 months, envelopes stay legible where a member-by-member swarm wouldn't.

The forecast has skill for a given lead when its navy ribbon sits clearly inside one climatology band rather than spanning all three. The bottom bars quantify that directly: what share of the ensemble falls below, within, or above the climatology-normal range — the operational probability of a below-, normal-, or above-normal outcome.

Verified skill

Every site is verified against a 31-year hindcast (1995–2025), averaged across all twelve initialisation months, using CRPSS (continuous ranked probability skill score, vs. climatology) and NSE (Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency).

Published skill criterion: NSE > 0 & CRPSS ≥ 0.1
Sites that fail it are shown as low- or no-skill rather than hidden. In practice: system storage is genuinely predictable (CRPSS 0.86 at t+1, still 0.14 at t+7 — a reservoir integrates its own history). Inflow skill is positive only at t+1; beyond that, climatology is as good as the forecast. Gate temperature reproduces the right seasonal shape but does not yet beat climatology (CRPSS below zero).

About NEREIDA

Built by Daniel Mercado-Bettín and Rafael Marcé at the Centre d'Estudis Avançats de Blanes (CEAB–CSIC). Applied here to Sau and Susqueda, the tandem reservoirs on the River Ter that supply drinking water to the Barcelona metropolitan area — a system pushed to record lows during the 2021–2024 Catalan drought. Both reservoirs are managed by the Agència Catalana de l'Aigua (ACA), part of the NEREIDA project, grant RDI001/24/000044. Source code: github.com/NEREIDACA/main.
